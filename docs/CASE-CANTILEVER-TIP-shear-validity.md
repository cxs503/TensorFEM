## 1. Case

Prismatic cantilever, rectangular section `b = 10 mm`, `h = 10 mm`,
`E = 200 GPa`, `nu = 0.3`, tip load `P = 5 N`, shear factor `kappa = 0.8333`.
Length is varied so that the slenderness sweeps `L/h = 100` down to `4`
(the section is held fixed, so only the slenderness changes).
The slender rung (`L/h = 100`) reproduces `benchmarks/cantilever_tip_deflection.py`.

Oracles (Timoshenko, *Phil. Mag.* 41 (1921) 744; Timoshenko & Gere, *Mechanics of
Materials*; Cowper, *J. Appl. Mech.* 33 (1966) 335 for the refined shear factor):

    Euler-Bernoulli   delta_EB = P L^3 / (3 E I)
    Timoshenko        delta_TI = P L^3 / (3 E I) + P L / (kappa G A)

Both are re-derived inside the driver by Castigliano's unit-load theorem with sympy
(residual `0.0e+00`), so the oracle is
machine-derived rather than transcribed.

## 2. Method

Three models of the same library are graded on every rung against the oracle that is
valid there:

* `frame_eb` -- `tensorfem.frame2d`, 2-node cubic Hermite Euler-Bernoulli frame (shear-rigid).
* `beam_timoshenko` -- `tensorfem.advanced_beam`, 2-node locking-free Timoshenko beam.
* `solid_hex20` -- `tensorfem.hex20`, 3-D HEX20 continuum (no beam kinematics), two
  meshes per rung, fully clamped root face, tip load spread over the end face.

The oracles separate by `(delta_TI - delta_EB)/delta_EB = E/(4 kappa G) (h/L)^2`, i.e.
3.00 % at the crossover
`L/h = 5.099`; below that slenderness Euler-Bernoulli is
outside the 3 % case-pool tolerance and must not be used as the reference.

## 5. Notes and limitations

* frame_eb and beam_timoshenko are nodally exact for a tip load, so their gates are consistency checks of stiffness, load vector and boundary conditions, not accuracy or convergence proofs.
* The HEX20 model clamps all three displacement components on the root face; that is stiffer than the idealised built-in of beam theory (no root warping, no Poisson contraction). The measured residual at the rungs where shear is negligible (L/h >= 20) is between -0.40 % and -0.38 % (the solid is stiffer than Euler-Bernoulli there), and it accounts for part of the residual at the thick rung.
* Timoshenko beam theory itself degrades for L/h < 5 (higher-order cross-section deformation, Timoshenko & Goodier, *Theory of Elasticity* 3rd ed. ch. 3); the L/h = 4 rung is at the edge of that validity range and is reported, not claimed as exact.
* kappa = 0.8333 (average-strain, rectangular section). Cowper's refined value 0.8496 for nu = 0.3 changes the shear term by 1.9 %, i.e. the total deflection by 0.09 % at the thick rung - far below the tolerance, so the choice does not move any verdict.
* The 2x2 / 3x3 quadratic cross-section meshes cannot represent the parabolic shear distribution exactly; the refinement trend (shear recovery rising with mesh density) is reported instead of a converged value, and the shear-recovery gate is deliberately loose (>= 0.5) for that reason.
  **Update (cross-section convergence, `docs/CASE-CANTILEVER-TIP-cross-section-convergence.md`)**: the limitation above is now measured. With a sparse solve (up to 6.6e4 dof, certified against this file's numbers to 3e-12) the recovery keeps rising under section refinement on an isotropic family, so 0.63 is not a converged value and, being rung-dependent (+0.03 at `L/h = 4` vs -0.00 at `L/h = 20`), it is **not** a measurement of `kappa`. The verdicts in §6 are unchanged. The residual attributed in the next bullet to the clamped root is dominated by discretisation instead: it halves at `L/h = 20` (-0.380% -> -0.180%) and changes sign at `L/h = 10` (-0.146% -> +0.029%).
* The tip load is spread uniformly over the end-face nodes (zero net moment), not applied as the exact parabolic shear traction of the Timoshenko solution; the difference is a Saint-Venant local field that decays within ~h of the tip and does not scale with L.
