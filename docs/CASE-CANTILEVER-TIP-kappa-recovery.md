## 1. Why (the debt this closes)

The shear ladder grades the HEX20 continuum against `delta_TI = PL^3/3EI + PL/(kappa G A)`
with `kappa = 5/6` and reports `shear_recovery = (delta_solid - delta_EB)/(delta_TI - delta_EB)`
= 0.63 at `L/h = 4`, gated loosely at `>= 0.5`.  That ratio cannot distinguish three
different residuals that all live in the same number: a flexural-stiffness error of the
mesh (`~L^3`), the clamped-root/load-introduction end effect (`~L^0`), and the shear
compliance itself (`~L`).  This driver separates them by fitting (F) with three
coefficients on a sweep where the *element aspect ratio* is held fixed.

## 2. Method

* Section `b = 10 mm`, `h = 10 mm`, `E = 200 GPa`, `nu = 0.3`, `P = 5.0 N`, `kappa = 0.8333` (`5/6`).
* Sweep `L/h = 4.0, 5.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, 12.0, 16.0, 20.0`,
  HEX20 mesh `(2*(L/h), 2, 2)`, fully clamped root face, tip load
  spread over the end face (zero net moment).
* Fit `delta = a PL^3/3EI + b PL/(kappa G A) + c`, i.e. `e = delta/delta_EB - 1`
  `= (a-1) + (b-1) 0.78 (h/L)^2 + C (h/L)^3` by linear least squares (exact for (F)).
* Controls: finer cross-section mesh at `L/h = 10`, and an axially refined mesh.
* Independent oracle: the exact plane-stress elasticity solution derived in-file from the
  Airy function `P*c**2*x*y/(2*I) + P*y**3*(L - x)/(6*I)` -- biharmonic residual `0`, equilibrium residuals `0` / `0`, strain compatibility `0`, flank tractions `['0', '0']`, end normal stress `0`, shear resultant `-P` (`-P`).

## 6. Notes / limitations

* The end offset `c` is *not* attributable further into 'clamped root' vs 'load introduction' here: separating those needs a support-face traction BC, which the clamped model does not have.
  It is reported as one L-independent constant, which is what the data support.
* The fit ladder stops at `L/h = 20`: above that the shear signal (`0.78 (h/L)^2`) falls below 0.2 %, i.e. under the 0.4 % flexural discretisation floor, and would only add noise to `b`.
* Timoshenko beam theory with any single kappa is a 1-D model; the comparison above is
  therefore about *which* 1-D coefficient the continuum agrees with, not about beam theory
  being exact.  For `L/h < 5` the 3-D field itself departs from any 1-D model.
* Only one section (`b = h`) and one Poisson ratio are probed; `kappa_eff` is expected to be
  `nu`-dependent (Cowper 0.8496 is quoted for `nu = 0.3`), which is out of scope here.

## 8. Disposition

The converged sweep gives `kappa_eff = 0.6854 +- 0.0540` (nearest theory value: section-centre 2/3 at 0.6667, 2.8 % away).  The bending carrier is recovered to `a = 0.99529` and the L-independent offset is `c = 1.545e-08 m`; `corr(a, c) = +0.774` shows why the two trade against each other and why `sigma_kappa` rather than the residual alone is the honest precision of the measured coefficient.

Consequences for the published ladder:

* The Timoshenko oracle of `run.py` is built on `kappa = 5/6`.  If the continuum really follows `0.6854`, the shear term of that oracle is off by `-17.8 %`, i.e. by `-0.87 %` of the deflection at `L/h = 4` -- which is the same size as the residual the ladder reports against its own oracle.
* Therefore the ladder's `solid vs Timoshenko` residual at the thick rung is *not* evidence of a missing shear degree of freedom in the solid; it is the arithmetic sum of a bending-flexibility ratio `a - 1 = -0.47 %` and a kappa-convention difference, and the `shear_recovery` ratio of the ladder (0.63) must not be read as '63 % of the shear compliance recovered'.

**Honest FAIL.**  Two of the pre-registered gates fail: `kappa_eff_within_10pct_of_energy_5_6` and `kappa_eff_not_centroid_value`. The hypothesis that the clamped HEX20 continuum recovers the strain-energy shear coefficient `5/6` is therefore **falsified** by this measurement at this mesh family -- the measured value is `0.6854`, closest to the section-centre reading `2/3` (2.8 % away, 0.3 sigma). `holdout_prediction` also fails, so the 3-parameter model (F) does not generalise to the slender rungs within 0.1 pp -- it is a local (thick-regime) decomposition, not a global law.  None of this changes the anchor's own `< 3 %` deflection verdicts in the companion document, which stand as published.

Next step this measurement points to: repeat the fit with an independently pinned bending ratio `a` (pure tip moment, zero shear force) so that `b` and `c` are fitted in a two-parameter design; that removes the `a`/`c` degeneracy and would shrink `sigma_kappa` below the 17.8 % gap between `5/6` and the measurement.
