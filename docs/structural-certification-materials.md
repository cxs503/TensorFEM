## Acceptance criterion

Relative-error tolerance **3.00 %** of the reference value. A row is `WITHHELD` when `|reference|` is below 1.00 % of the stress scale, because the relative criterion is then ill-conditioned; withheld rows are never counted as passes.

> The hole-edge peak is a nodal-averaged recovered quantity: its ladder is not monotone and overshoots 3T by O(1e-3), so no GCI is claimed for it (WITHHELD, ladder envelope reported). The pointwise hoop stress at r/a=1.05 does satisfy the GCI preconditions and gets an analytic band.
> WITHHELD rows are those whose |reference| falls below 1 % of the far-field tension (the shear component on both symmetry rays, where the exact value is identically zero). A relative-error criterion is meaningless there; the absolute residual is reported in the table instead of a fabricated percentage.
> The Kirsch case is traction driven, so its stress field is independent of E; the Poisson sweep probes the sensitivity of the FE solution to nu, not a change of the reference solution.
> The combined half-width is a linear (worst case) sum of the discretisation and parametric contributions. It is not a statistical confidence interval and must not be reported as one.
> Pointwise radial stress fails the 3 % gate at three of the six probes per ray even at the finest level. That is a real limitation of bilinear Q4 stress recovery inside the near-hole gradient layer, where the exact radial stress vanishes at r=a; it is reported as FAIL rather than dropped, and it is why the certification claim is scoped to the hoop stress / stress concentration and not to every component.
> The finite-width baseline is the Howland (1930) series as tabulated in Peterson's stress concentration charts, with the Heywood (1952) fit, whose own accuracy is about 2 %, as a secondary cross-check.
